  <section id="information-chineseecho-handles">
    <h2>1. Information ChineseEcho handles</h2>
    <p>ChineseEcho handles information that you enter or create while using the app. This may include vocabulary, pinyin, meanings, tags, learner hints, dictation sets, contextual sentences, practice results, review schedules, study preferences, and active-session progress.</p>
    <p>This information is stored locally on your Mac using Apple’s SwiftData framework. It is not uploaded to the developer.</p>
  </section>

  <section id="information-the-developer-collects">
    <h2>2. Information the developer collects</h2>
    <p>ChineseEcho does not send the developer your learning data, usage history, device identifiers, or diagnostic analytics. The app does not include advertising, cross-app tracking, or developer-operated analytics services.</p>
    <p>If you contact support by email, the developer receives the information you choose to include in that message. Support correspondence is used only to respond to your request and investigate the issue you report.</p>
  </section>

  <section id="local-storage-and-backups">
    <h2>3. Local storage and backups</h2>
    <p>Your library, practice history, generated content, and preferences remain in the app’s local macOS container. ChineseEcho does not automatically synchronize this information to a ChineseEcho cloud service.</p>
    <p>When you export a backup, ChineseEcho creates a JSON file at the location you select through the macOS save panel. You control that file and any copies or synchronization performed by the destination you choose, including iCloud Drive or another third-party storage provider.</p>
  </section>

  <section id="speech-and-notifications">
    <h2>4. Speech and notifications</h2>
    <p>ChineseEcho uses native macOS speech services to read dictation content aloud. Speech content is handled by the operating system according to your Mac’s configuration; ChineseEcho does not send that content to a developer-operated server.</p>
    <p>If you allow notifications, ChineseEcho schedules due-review reminders locally through macOS. Notification permission can be changed at any time in <strong>System Settings → Notifications → ChineseEcho</strong>.</p>
  </section>

  <section id="optional-local-ai">
    <h2>5. Optional Local AI</h2>
    <p>Local AI is optional. If you choose to install it, ChineseEcho downloads model files from Hugging Face. The download request may provide Hugging Face with standard network information such as your IP address, request time, and user-agent information. Hugging Face handles that information under its own privacy terms.</p>
    <p>After the model is downloaded, ChineseEcho performs supported vocabulary enrichment and contextual-sentence generation on your Mac. The learning content submitted to the local model is not sent to the developer.</p>
  </section>

  <section id="file-access-and-system-permissions">
    <h2>6. File access and system permissions</h2>
    <p>ChineseEcho requests access only when a feature needs it. File access is limited to locations you select through standard macOS open and save panels. Notification access is used for local review reminders. The app does not request access to your contacts, photos, microphone, camera, or location.</p>
  </section>

  <section id="sharing-and-sale-of-information">
    <h2>7. Sharing and sale of information</h2>
    <p>The developer does not sell, rent, or share your learning data because ChineseEcho does not receive it. ChineseEcho does not use personal information for targeted advertising or profiling.</p>
  </section>

  <section id="retention-and-deletion">
    <h2>8. Retention and deletion</h2>
    <p>Local learning data remains on your Mac until you delete it, replace it through a restore operation, or remove the app’s stored container. You can delete vocabulary and dictation sets within ChineseEcho. Downloaded model files can be removed from <strong>Settings → Local AI → Remove Model</strong>.</p>
    <p>Backup files remain wherever you saved them until you delete them. Removing the ChineseEcho application alone may not remove its local data container or exported backups.</p>
  </section>

  <section id="security">
    <h2>9. Security</h2>
    <p>ChineseEcho relies on macOS application sandboxing and system file-selection controls to limit access to local resources. No method of storage is guaranteed to be completely secure, so you should protect your Mac account and keep macOS up to date.</p>
  </section>

  <section id="childrens-privacy">
    <h2>10. Children’s privacy</h2>
    <p>ChineseEcho is suitable for learners of different ages, but it does not knowingly collect personal information from children or other users. Parents, guardians, and schools remain responsible for managing the Mac, exported backups, and any support email sent on a learner’s behalf.</p>
  </section>

  <section id="third-party-services">
    <h2>11. Third-party services</h2>
    <p>The optional model download is hosted by Hugging Face, and the public ChineseEcho website and release downloads are hosted by GitHub. Visiting those services or downloading files from them is subject to their respective privacy policies. ChineseEcho does not control those services’ data practices.</p>
  </section>

  <section id="changes-to-this-policy">
    <h2>12. Changes to this policy</h2>
    <p>This policy may be revised when ChineseEcho’s features, distribution, or data practices change. The effective date at the top of this page identifies the current version. Material changes will be reflected on this page.</p>
  </section>
